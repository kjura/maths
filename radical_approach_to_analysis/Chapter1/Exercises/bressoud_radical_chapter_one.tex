\section*{Exercise 1.2.6}

Consider Fourier's series:

\begin{equation*}
        f(x) = \frac{4}{\pi}\Big[\cos \frac{\pi x}{2} - \frac{1}{3} \cos \frac{3\pi x}{2} + \frac{1}{5} \cos \frac{5\pi x}{2} - \frac{1}{7} \cos \frac{7\pi x}{2} + \dots \Big]
\end{equation*}


\subsection*{a.}

Looking at the graph of Fourier's series we can say that the the limit as
\(x\) approaches \(1\) from the left is \(1\).

\subsection*{b.}

Looking at the graph of Fourier's series we can say that the the limit as
\(x\) approaches \(1\) from the right is \(-1\).

\subsection*{c.}

Looking at the graph of Fourier's series we can say that the value of \(f(1)\)
is \(0\).
